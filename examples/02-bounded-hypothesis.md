# Example: a bounded hypothesis

All names and events below are synthetic.

## Input

> Crystallize the useful insight.
>
> One participant said a required checklist interrupted the report they wanted
> to submit immediately. They skipped it when allowed and then submitted the
> report. No comparison group, completion metric, or second participant exists.

## Output

Outcome: CRYSTALLIZE

# Required checklists may delay immediate-goal users

## Core Insight
For a task-ready user with an immediate submission goal, a required checklist may delay goal-directed action. This is a testable hypothesis from one report, not a general effect.

## Type
Hypothesis

## Why It Matters
It identifies a specific onboarding risk without turning one participant's experience into a universal rule.

## Evidence
One synthetic participant reported interruption, skipped the checklist when allowed, and then submitted the report. No comparison group, completion metric, replication, or population evidence was supplied.

## Mechanism
Unconfirmed chain: immediate goal -> required checklist interrupts the path -> goal-directed action is delayed -> completion may be affected.

## Application
- When: Evaluating required pre-task checklists for task-ready users.
- Do: Compare required and skippable variants with a defined completion measure.
- Observe: Interruption, time to intended action, bypass behavior, and completion.

## Falsification
The hypothesis weakens if required checklists do not delay action or reduce completion in comparable tests. Revise the pathway if completion changes for reasons unrelated to delayed goal-directed action.

## Boundaries
The evidence covers one synthetic participant with an immediate goal. It does not establish effects for unfamiliar users, users without an immediate goal, optional checklists, or users generally.

## Source
Current user-supplied synthetic participant note; not independently verified.
