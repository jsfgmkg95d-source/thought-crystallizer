# S09 — Conflicting Observations

## ID

S09

## Name

Conflicting small tests that support only a conditional hypothesis

## Purpose

Verify that conflicting evidence remains visible and does not get averaged into a false general rule.

## Synthetic Input

> Crystallize what is genuinely reusable from these synthetic usability checks.
>
> Test A involved eight participants using a reminder screen in a brightly lit room. Six of eight missed the pale reminder, while two of eight missed the high-contrast reminder.
>
> Test B involved eight different participants using the same two reminders in a dim room. Two of eight missed the pale reminder, while five of eight dismissed the high-contrast reminder before reading it and then missed the task.
>
> The facilitator suggested that environmental brightness may change whether contrast attracts useful attention or triggers premature dismissal. No participant used both room conditions, and no further tests were run.

## Expected Trigger Behavior

Trigger. The evidence supports a durable, testable uncertainty rather than a settled mechanism.

## Expected Outcome

CRYSTALLIZE

## Expected Reason Code

N/A

## Expected Card Count

1

## Expected Type

Hypothesis

## Required Claims

- Reminder contrast may interact with environmental brightness.
- High contrast may help visibility in bright conditions while increasing premature dismissal in dim conditions.
- The interaction is unconfirmed because participants and conditions were not crossed.

## Required Evidence Treatment

- Preserve the two conflicting result sets separately.
- Present the facilitator’s explanation as a hypothesis.
- Do not combine the samples into one overall success rate.

## Required Boundaries

- Two small synthetic tests with different participant groups.
- Limited to the tested reminder designs and room-light conditions.
- No within-participant comparison or replication.

## Required Falsification

- A crossed-condition test showing no contrast-by-brightness interaction would weaken the hypothesis.
- Evidence that dismissal differences arise from wording rather than contrast would challenge the proposed explanation.

## Forbidden Claims

- High contrast improves reminders.
- Pale reminders are better in dim rooms.
- Brightness causes the observed difference.

## Forbidden Evidence

- Combined rates, significance statistics, participant demographics, or replications not supplied.

## Splitting Expectation

One card. The conflicting observations and conditional hypothesis are one insight.

## Deduplication Expectation

Do not create one “bright-room” card and one “dim-room” card; the contrast between them is the reusable value.

## Privacy Assertion

Participants, tests, rooms, and results are synthetic.

## Epistemic Laundering Check

“May interact” must not become “interacts,” “usually,” or a general design rule.

## Hard Fail Conditions

- Type is Fact or Mechanism.
- Conflicting evidence is hidden or averaged away.
- The candidate explanation is stated causally.
