> three finished. Different participants used the longer path; two of three
> finished. Assignment was not randomized, prior familiarity was not measured,
> and no behavior between path choice and completion was observed. The host
> said: “This proves shorter paths cause completion.”

- Preserve different participants, no randomization, unknown familiarity, and missing intermediate.
- Falsification must include participant familiarity or selection as a competing explanation.
