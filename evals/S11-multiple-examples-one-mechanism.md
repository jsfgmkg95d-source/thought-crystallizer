# S11 — Multiple Examples, One Mechanism

## ID

S11

## Name

Two independent examples supporting the same bottleneck mechanism

## Purpose

Verify that examples become evidence within one crystal rather than separate cards when they share one variable chain.

## Synthetic Input

> Crystallize the reusable insight from these two synthetic workflow reviews.
>
> Example One: In the Cedar Catalog exercise, every text correction required approval from one editor. Over four rounds, the editor’s queue grew from two to eleven items. Seven authors were idle while waiting. When approval authority was assigned to one editor per catalog section in a replay, the longest queue fell to three items and authors resumed without waiting for the central editor.
>
> Example Two: In the Harbor Labels exercise, every label correction also required one coordinator. The coordinator’s queue reached nine items while four reviewers were idle. In a second run, approval was delegated by label category. The longest queue fell to two items without changing the review checklist.
>
> No evidence was collected for regulated work or changes requiring cross-section consistency.

## Expected Trigger Behavior

Trigger. The two examples support the same condition–intermediate–outcome chain.

## Expected Outcome

CRYSTALLIZE

## Expected Reason Code

N/A

## Expected Card Count

1

## Expected Type

Mechanism

## Required Claims

- Centralizing every low-level approval in one person can create a queue bottleneck when other qualified reviewers remain idle.
- Delegating approval by bounded category reduced the central queue in both supplied exercises.

## Required Evidence Treatment

- Retain both exercises as mutually supporting observations.
- Identify queue growth and idle reviewers as intermediate evidence.
- Do not claim that delegation preserved quality; only the checklist was unchanged.

## Required Boundaries

- Low-level corrections with separable catalog sections or label categories.
- Excludes regulated work and decisions requiring cross-category consistency.
- Based only on two synthetic exercises.

## Required Falsification

- If category delegation leaves queue length and waiting unchanged under comparable load, the proposed bottleneck mechanism is weakened.
- If quality failures arise specifically from lost cross-category consistency, the application boundary must narrow.

## Forbidden Claims

- Decentralized approval is always faster or better.
- Quality was unchanged.
- Central review should be removed from regulated or coupled work.

## Forbidden Evidence

- Quality scores, regulatory outcomes, additional exercises, or employee data.

## Splitting Expectation

One card. The two exercises are evidence for one mechanism.

## Deduplication Expectation

Do not generate separate Cedar and Harbor cards.

## Privacy Assertion

Both exercises, organizations, roles, and measurements are synthetic.

## Epistemic Laundering Check

Two supporting exercises do not establish universal effectiveness or unchanged quality.

## Hard Fail Conditions

- Two cards are generated.
- Quality preservation is asserted without evidence.
- The stated exclusions are lost.
