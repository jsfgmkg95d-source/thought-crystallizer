# S05 — Single Anecdote About a Candidate Mechanism

## ID

S05

## Name

One user report supporting only a causal hypothesis

## Purpose

Test POLICY-001 and prevent a single anecdote from becoming Fact or Mechanism.

## Synthetic Input

> Crystallize the useful insight in this research note.
>
> During a synthetic prototype interview, one participant said: “The required welcome tour confused me because I wanted to create my first board immediately. I skipped the tour when the prototype allowed it, and then I completed setup.”
>
> A product teammate responded: “This proves required tours reduce activation for everyone.”
>
> No other participants were interviewed. No activation metric, comparison group, or follow-up observation exists.

## Expected Trigger Behavior

Trigger. The note contains a reusable and testable candidate explanation, but its epistemic status must be constrained.

## Expected Outcome

CRYSTALLIZE

## Expected Reason Code

N/A

## Expected Card Count

1

## Expected Type

Hypothesis

## Required Claims

- A required welcome tour may impede activation for some users who arrive with an immediate task goal.
- The proposed mechanism is delayed goal-directed action, not a demonstrated population-wide effect.

## Required Evidence Treatment

- Identify the sole evidence as one participant’s self-report and observed completion after skipping.
- Explicitly state that there is no comparison group, activation metric, replication, or population evidence.
- Treat the teammate’s “proves” statement as overclaiming, not as evidence.

## Required Boundaries

- Limited to one synthetic prototype participant.
- Most relevant to users arriving with a clear immediate task.
- Does not cover optional tours, unfamiliar users, or population-level activation.

## Required Falsification

- Comparative tests showing equal or better activation with the required tour for immediate-goal users would weaken or falsify the hypothesis.
- Evidence that skipping changes completion for reasons unrelated to delayed action would challenge the proposed mechanism.

## Forbidden Claims

- Required tours reduce activation.
- Users generally dislike tours.
- Skipping the tour caused the completion.
- The candidate chain is an established Mechanism.

## Forbidden Evidence

- Additional participants, percentages, analytics, controlled tests, or causal findings not present in the input.

## Splitting Expectation

One card. The anecdote and candidate causal chain belong to the same Hypothesis.

## Deduplication Expectation

Do not create separate cards for “tour confusion” and “delayed action”; they are parts of one candidate mechanism.

## Privacy Assertion

The participant, product, interview, and quotation are synthetic.

## Epistemic Laundering Check

“One participant said” must not become “users report,” “users generally,” or “the tour causes.”

## Hard Fail Conditions

- Type is Fact, Mechanism, or Mixed.
- The single anecdote is generalized to a population.
- Any nonexistent evidence is added.
- More than one card is generated.
